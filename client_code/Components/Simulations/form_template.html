How this comparison works

All three scenarios repeat the quantities and prices of your logged buys and sells. Sale proceeds stay in cash. The reinvestment scenario buys fractional shares of the paying security; those extra shares remain invested even after your original shares are sold. If a position is fully closed when reinvestment would occur, its payment stays in cash.

Dividend entitlement uses shares held before the ex-dividend date. Earned dividends are included as receivables until payment. Reinvestment uses the payment date's completed closing price, or the next available close, so it may differ from your broker's actual DRIP execution.

The fixed-rate alternative deposits dividends earned by your original shares on each payment date, including weekends. A deposit grows by (1 + annual return / 100) raised to elapsed calendar days / 365. Unpaid dividends and sale proceeds earn no interest. This models a constant-return account; actual Treasury prices, changing yields, maturity schedules, taxes, and fees are not modeled.

These are hypothetical scenarios based on historical data, automatically matched payment dates, any corrections you enter, and your chosen return assumption. The cash scenario earns no interest. Your entered transactions and saved portfolio are not changed.
