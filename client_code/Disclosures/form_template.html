How the numbers work

Disclosures & methods

Understand the assumptions behind your portfolio readout, simulations, and retirement projections. These tools provide information for exploring scenarios; they do not promise investment results.

Portfolio analysis

Performance & dividends

Total return in Overview is investment profit divided by total buy capital. The charts use time-weighted performance, adjusting for purchases as contributions and sales as withdrawals.

With no deposit ledger or execution times, buys are treated as beginning-of-session inflows and sells as end-of-session outflows. The first price mark establishes the comparison baseline; investment profit still includes your entered execution prices.

Dividends are credited on ex-dividend dates, before tax, without reinvestment. Weekend or holiday trades use the next available trading session. Today's regular-session quote is provisional. Annualized return is withheld while the endpoint is provisional.

Volatility & Sharpe ratios

Volatility is the sample standard deviation of daily returns × √252. Sharpe is mean daily excess return divided by its standard deviation × √252, using your annual risk-free rate converted to a daily rate.

Both portfolio and benchmark risk need at least 20 completed daily returns; the unfinished session is excluded. Benchmark risk uses the selected price or total-return series over matching chart dates.

Sessions with no invested portfolio capital are excluded only from portfolio risk. Annualized portfolio return is not shown when history contains these gaps or a provisional endpoint.

Market data

Analysis uses daily history and timestamped regular-session quotes, plus dividends, splits, and benchmarks. Quotes may be delayed and historical coverage may be incomplete. Entered execution prices take precedence; otherwise the latest session price is used for a current-day trade. Historical trades use their daily close. Pre-market and after-hours prices are excluded. Analysis uses USD-listed US securities.

Dividend reinvestment simulations · Beta

Three paths through the same history

Reinvest DRIP replays the selected securities from your synced portfolio using the same logged buy and sell quantities and execution prices in all three scenarios. Every Buy is treated as new contributed capital; remove already-logged DRIP purchases from a working copy before simulating to avoid double-counting reinvestment.

The cash scenario retains dividends and sale proceeds as cash earning no interest. The DRIP scenario buys fractional shares with paid dividends. Extra DRIP shares remain after a sale of the original share quantity and can earn later dividends. If no shares remain when a payment can be reinvested, that payment stays in cash.

The fixed-rate account scenario keeps the same security shares as the cash scenario and deposits their dividends into a separate account on each confirmed payment date. You choose an effective annual return between 0% and 100%. The initial 4% is an illustrative assumption you can change. Sale proceeds remain idle cash, and unpaid dividends earn no return.

Each deposit grows by (1 + annual rate / 100) raised to (elapsed calendar days / 365), through the same completed-session endpoint as the other scenarios. This includes weekends and holidays, uses a 365-day basis even in leap years, and reinvests the account's earnings. A payment has zero accrued interest on its deposit date. At 0%, this scenario equals keeping dividends in cash.

This constant-rate model can illustrate an assumed Treasury-style or savings-account return. It does not model Treasury auctions, maturities, purchase increments, changing yields or market-price movements. Treasury bills pay their face value at maturity, with interest determined by the purchase price. See TreasuryDirect's Treasury bill explanation.

The comparison shows total wealth: securities, retained cash, and earned but unpaid dividends. Profit is total wealth minus buy capital. This is not the time-weighted return chart in Performance, where sales are treated as withdrawals. Simulated transactions do not alter your saved portfolio.

Dates, prices, and coverage

Entitlement is based on shares held before trades on the ex-dividend date. Cash becomes available on the payment date. Reinvestment uses that day's completed closing price, or the next available trading close. This is an assumed fill; a broker's actual DRIP execution can differ. Earned dividends awaiting payment remain receivables, and a payment awaiting a completed trading close remains cash.

Historical prices, splits, dividend amounts, and payment dates are matched automatically. A payment-date record must match the security, ex-date, USD currency, and cash amount. Payment dates and any user corrections are shown in the optional dividend review. An ex-date is never used as a substitute for a payment date.

History coverage varies by security and may be shorter than the simulation period. If an earned dividend's payment date cannot be confirmed, the comparison pauses with a retry option and an optional broker-confirmed correction. Scheduled payment dates are modeled as the cash-availability dates; actual broker crediting and execution may differ.

The model uses completed daily closes over up to ten years, with splits applied to shares and historical amounts kept in the units used at that time. Transactions without a completed trading close cannot be simulated yet. Special distributions with payment dates before ex-dates (due bills) are unsupported. Missing prices or required dates stop the calculation instead of estimating a return.

Taxes, transaction fees and broker-specific reinvestment rules are excluded. Only the fixed-rate dividend account earns the assumed interest; other cash earns zero. Results combine historical security data with your hypothetical account rate. Scenarios, the rate and entered payment dates are not saved to your account.

Dividend income & loss review

Dividend estimates and upcoming dates

Income uses the securities selected in your synced analysis and up to ten years of logged transactions. Annual income repeats the trailing twelve months of per-share cash distributions, adjusted for splits, multiplied by the shares held on the analysis date. It includes special distributions and assumes no dividend growth, cuts or reinvestment. The average quarterly estimate is annual income divided by four; it is not a dated payment forecast. Shorter histories are identified and are not scaled up to a full year.

Yield on cost divides estimated annual income by the remaining FIFO purchase cost of open holdings. This differs from current yield, which uses market value. Missing purchase prices may use same-day market prices and are marked as estimates. Unknown basis makes yield unavailable rather than treating cost as zero.

The payment history uses shares held before ex-date trades. It includes entitlements retained after a position was sold. Expected cash totals use confirmed payment dates matched to the ex-date and cash amount. Unconfirmed payment dates and unsupported due-bill distributions are excluded from received-cash totals. These are modeled receipts, not reconciled broker cash. Taxes, withholding, cash interest and bond coupons are excluded. Reinvested shares appear only when their purchases are logged.

Upcoming ex-dates use the latest available announcements, even if a historical analysis date is selected. We do not invent future ex-dates from a dividend schedule. Future cash estimates assume the snapshot shares, adjusted for known subsequent splits, remain held through the ex-date. Amounts and dates can change.

US taxable-account loss review

Open purchase lots and year-to-date realized gains use FIFO and split-adjusted quantities. A holding can contain losing lots even if the holding is profitable overall. Specific-lot sales may require identification with your broker; its basis and lot selection take precedence. The review does not apply wash-sale basis adjustments, return-of-capital adjustments, fees, transfers, gifts or inherited-basis rules.

Selected unflagged unrealized losses are compared with positive net realized gains in the selected, logged history. This is a potential capital-gain offset, not a deductible-loss determination or estimated tax saving. Missing realized basis suppresses the offset calculation. Carryforwards, capital gain distributions and other accounts are not included. The review is for US taxable accounts, not losses within IRAs or other tax-advantaged accounts.

The wash-sale screen flags same-symbol purchases logged within thirty days before or after the analysis date. It cannot establish that a sale is clear: future purchases, DRIP, a spouse's accounts, IRAs and substantially identical securities may affect deductibility. Flagged losses are excluded from the offset estimate until reviewed. Confirm tax lots and treatment with your broker and tax adviser.

The sale simulator separately combines selected quantities of winning and losing positions using FIFO, LIFO or HIFO against the remaining FIFO lots at the snapshot date. It shows modeled short-term and long-term gains, losses and netting before wash-sale adjustments. Its modeled losses and offsets may not be deductible as shown. Missing selected-lot basis blocks the scenario; missing realized basis prevents year-to-date netting. Carryforwards, fees, special asset rules and tax rates are excluded. LIFO/HIFO require appropriate lot identification with the broker. The simulator does not estimate tax owed or place trades.

References: IRS capital gains and losses and IRS Publication 550: basis, holding periods and wash sales.

Foreign exchange

Reference rates and scenarios

Forex uses dated daily currency reference rates from the European Central Bank through Frankfurter. These are indicative reference values, not live bid/ask quotes or executable prices. Weekends and publication holidays may show the previous published business day. Each pair is expressed as units of the quote currency for one unit of the base currency.

Conversion and trade scenarios use the inputs shown in the Forex workspace. A long position gains when the quote price rises; a short position gains when it falls. Position size is expressed in units of the base currency, and profit/loss is expressed in the quote currency. Stop and target prices are hypothetical levels, not guaranteed fills. Check the scenario's stated cost assumptions against your broker's spread, fees and financing.

Scenarios are temporary planning tools and do not submit orders or connect to a trading account. Reference-rate movements do not reproduce a leveraged trading account's returns. See the ECB's reference-rate information and Frankfurter's data documentation.

Retirement projections

What the probability means

The calculator runs 2,000 hypothetical monthly return paths. A successful path funds every planned retirement withdrawal through the selected end age and meets the optional ending-balance goal. The displayed probability is the share of simulated paths that meet both conditions, conditional on your assumptions.

A 100% result means all sampled paths succeeded; it is not certainty. Simulation sampling also introduces uncertainty (roughly ±2 percentage points near a 50% result for 2,000 paths). The same random seed is used for repeatable scenario comparisons.

Purchasing power & cash flows

Balances, contributions, retirement spending, other income, and the ending-balance goal are expressed in today's US dollars. Contributions, spending, and other income therefore rise with assumed inflation in nominal dollars. Other income begins at the retirement age; it is a user-entered assumption, not a Social Security or pension estimate.

During saving years, contributions arrive at the end of each month after growth. At retirement, the monthly spending shortfall after other income is withdrawn at the start of each month, before growth. Any income above spending is invested. A path that cannot fund a scheduled withdrawal remains unsuccessful. Exactly funding the final withdrawal can succeed with a zero ending-balance goal.

Returns, volatility & fees

Expected return is the assumed arithmetic mean annual total return, including reinvested distributions, before fees and inflation. Volatility is the annual standard deviation of simple returns. Monthly independent lognormal returns are calibrated to those annual assumptions; balances are adjusted for inflation and an annual asset-based fee, compounded monthly.

The initial return, volatility, inflation, and fee values are examples for exploration, not recommendations or forecasts. Importing your selected holdings' value changes only the starting balance. The model does not infer future growth or risk from those holdings or their past performance.

Reading the chart & limitations

The chart shows pointwise 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentile balances at each age, including unsuccessful paths. Each percentile line combines outcomes across many paths; it is not an individual investment journey, and outcomes can fall outside the shaded bands.

The model assumes constant expected return and volatility, independent monthly returns, fixed real cash flows, and one portfolio return process. It does not model taxes, withdrawal rules, changing asset allocation, individual security concentration, market regimes, healthcare shocks, or uncertain lifespan beyond your chosen end age. Its return distribution may understate extreme losses. Revisit inputs as circumstances change.

Scenarios stay in the current portfolio workspace while you navigate; they are not saved to your account. Reloading or opening a different portfolio resets them.

Usage analytics and creator reporting

What is recorded

Folio records named page and tab visits, approximate country, and approximate active time. Signed-in visits are linked to the account for creator-only reporting; guest activity is counted by browsing visit. Browser Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control signals disable this analytics collection. The tracker uses the existing server session and does not add a browser tracking identifier.

Active-time measurements run while the app is visible and focused, and stop after two minutes without interaction. Only the timing of activity is used; input contents, keystrokes, screenshots and precise location are not recorded. Time is aggregated per visit and tab, not stored as an individual interaction log. Browser closure, lost connectivity and backgrounding can leave intervals uncounted, so these figures are estimates. Historical visits from before time tracking was introduced do not have measured duration.

The creator can review account and saved-portfolio information, account activity summaries and aggregate stock ownership. Community holdings use the most recently saved portfolio per account to avoid counting multiple saved scenarios as separate investors. They reflect entered portfolios rather than verified brokerage balances. Quoted market value, remaining entered purchase cost and their coverage are shown separately; missing prices or costs are not treated as zero. Creator reporting is restricted by server-side access checks.
