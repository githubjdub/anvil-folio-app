How the numbers work

Disclosures & methods

Understand the assumptions behind your portfolio readout and retirement projections. These tools provide information for exploring scenarios; they do not promise investment results.

Portfolio analysis

Performance & dividends

Total return in Overview is investment profit divided by total buy capital. The charts use time-weighted performance, adjusting for purchases as contributions and sales as withdrawals.

With no deposit ledger or execution times, buys are treated as beginning-of-session inflows and sells as end-of-session outflows. The first price mark establishes the comparison baseline; investment profit still includes your entered execution prices.

Dividends are credited on ex-dividend dates, before tax, without reinvestment. Weekend or holiday trades use the next available trading session. Today's regular-session quote is provisional. Annualized return is withheld while the endpoint is provisional.

Volatility & Sharpe ratios

Volatility is the sample standard deviation of daily returns × √252. Sharpe is mean daily excess return divided by its standard deviation × √252, using your annual risk-free rate converted to a daily rate.

Both portfolio and benchmark risk need at least 20 completed daily returns; the unfinished session is excluded. Benchmark risk uses the selected price or total-return series over matching chart dates.

Sessions with no invested portfolio capital are excluded only from portfolio risk. Annualized portfolio return is not shown when history contains these gaps or a provisional endpoint.

Data sources

Yahoo Finance supplies daily history and timestamped regular-session quotes, plus dividends, splits, and benchmarks. Quotes may be delayed. Entered execution prices take precedence; otherwise the latest session price is used for a current-day trade. Historical trades use their daily close. Pre-market and after-hours prices are excluded. Analysis uses USD-listed US securities.

Retirement projections

What the probability means

The calculator runs 2,000 hypothetical monthly return paths. A successful path funds every planned retirement withdrawal through the selected end age and meets the optional ending-balance goal. The displayed probability is the share of simulated paths that meet both conditions, conditional on your assumptions.

A 100% result means all sampled paths succeeded; it is not certainty. Simulation sampling also introduces uncertainty (roughly ±2 percentage points near a 50% result for 2,000 paths). The same random seed is used for repeatable scenario comparisons.

Purchasing power & cash flows

Balances, contributions, retirement spending, other income, and the ending-balance goal are expressed in today's US dollars. Contributions, spending, and other income therefore rise with assumed inflation in nominal dollars. Other income begins at the retirement age; it is a user-entered assumption, not a Social Security or pension estimate.

During saving years, contributions arrive at the end of each month after growth. At retirement, the monthly spending shortfall after other income is withdrawn at the start of each month, before growth. Any income above spending is invested. A path that cannot fund a scheduled withdrawal remains unsuccessful. Exactly funding the final withdrawal can succeed with a zero ending-balance goal.

Returns, volatility & fees

Expected return is the assumed arithmetic mean annual total return, including reinvested distributions, before fees and inflation. Volatility is the annual standard deviation of simple returns. Monthly independent lognormal returns are calibrated to those annual assumptions; balances are adjusted for inflation and an annual asset-based fee, compounded monthly.

The initial return, volatility, inflation, and fee values are examples for exploration, not recommendations or forecasts. Importing your selected holdings' value changes only the starting balance. The model does not infer future growth or risk from those holdings or their past performance.

Reading the chart & limitations

The chart shows pointwise 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentile balances at each age, including unsuccessful paths. Each percentile line combines outcomes across many paths; it is not an individual investment journey, and outcomes can fall outside the shaded bands.

The model assumes constant expected return and volatility, independent monthly returns, fixed real cash flows, and one portfolio return process. It does not model taxes, withdrawal rules, changing asset allocation, individual security concentration, market regimes, healthcare shocks, or uncertain lifespan beyond your chosen end age. Its return distribution may understate extreme losses. Revisit inputs as circumstances change.

Scenarios stay in the current portfolio workspace while you navigate; they are not saved to your account. Reloading or opening a different portfolio resets them.
